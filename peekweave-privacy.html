Privacy Policy

Peekweave is a hidden-object game from independent developer Andrew Kolmohorov. This policy describes the iOS and iPadOS version.

Game progress and settings

Peekweave stores your discovered objects, completed scenes, preferences, and ad frequency record on your device. A Peekweave account is unnecessary. The developer does not receive your game progress.

Advertising and privacy choices

The free game uses Google AdMob interstitial ads. The iOS app requests non-personalized ads. It does not request App Tracking Transparency permission or location permissions. It disables AdMob's publisher first-party identifier and automatic collection of device location. Google may infer a general location from your IP address.

Google Mobile Ads and Google User Messaging Platform may process IP address, general region, device or app information, identifiers available to the SDK under applicable settings, ad interactions, diagnostic information, and privacy choices. Google uses data to select and deliver ads, apply consent and opt-out choices, measure performance, and prevent abuse. See how Google uses data from partner apps and Google's Privacy Policy.

Where applicable, Google presents a regional consent or opt-out message. When the app offers an Ad Privacy button under Settings, use it to review or change your choices. The game remains playable when ads are unavailable.

Purchases

Apple processes the optional annual Ad-free subscription. Peekweave uses StoreKit to verify the entitlement and disable interstitial ads while it is active. In the Shop, use Restore Purchases with the Apple Account that subscribed, or Manage Subscription to change renewal settings with Apple. The developer does not receive your payment card details. See Apple's Privacy Policy.

Questions and requests

Contact about this policy or to exercise an applicable privacy right. If you email support, we use your email address and the details you provide to respond. For questions about Google's processing, use the controls or contact channels in Google's privacy policy.
